Cover-up triangles

Cover the thing you want to find. What's left tells you what to do: side by side = multiply, one on top of the other = divide.

  • V = I × R, I = V ÷ R, R = V ÷ I
  • P = I × V, I = P ÷ V, V = P ÷ I

Worked example

A washing machine draws a current of 12 A and has a resistance of 3 Ω. What is its power?

  1. Pick the formula that uses I and R: P = I² × R
  2. Square the current first: 12² = 12 × 12 = 144
  3. Multiply by R: 144 × 3 = 432
  4. Add the unit: P = 432 W

Common slip: 12 × 3 = 36 forgets to square the current.

Key facts to get right

  • Only electrons move between objects. Protons stay put in the nucleus.
  • Induction = a charged object is brought near, with no touching. The charges in the neutral object rearrange, and it stays neutral overall.
  • In a circuit, electrons flow from the negative (−) terminal to the positive (+) terminal.
  • Cell symbol: long plate = +, short plate = −.

Longer wire → more resistance. Thicker wire → less resistance. Higher-resistivity material → more resistance. Remember to change mm² to m² (÷ 1 000 000).

5. Inside a battery

  • A chemical reaction at the anode (−) releases electrons.
  • Electrons travel through the outside circuit (lighting the bulb) to the cathode (+).
  • Inside the battery, ions (not electrons) move through the electrolyte and the separator to balance the charge.
  • A battery dies when the chemicals are used up. Recharging pushes electrons back and reverses the reaction, but electrodes wear out a little each cycle.

7. Reading an experiment graph

Independent variable (IV): the thing you change on purpose. It goes on the x-axis. Here: voltage (V).

Dependent variable (DV): the thing you measure. It goes on the y-axis. Here: current (A).

Control variables: things kept the same so the test is fair, e.g. same resistor/wire, same temperature, same meters.

Relationship sentence pattern:
“As the IV increases, the DV increases / decreases.”

As voltage increases, current increases. Doubling the voltage (6 V → 12 V) doubles the current (0.8 A → 1.6 A).

8. Lightning crouch, and Van de Graaff vs wall socket

Caught outside with no shelter?

  • Crouch low on the balls of your feet, head tucked, hands over ears.
  • Keep feet together so you touch the ground as little as possible.
  • Stay away from trees, tall objects, water and metal. Drop the umbrella.
  • Find a low area but don't lie flat. Spread out from other people.
  • Why: lightning takes the shortest path to the ground, often through the tallest object.

Why a Van de Graaff is safe but a wall socket isn't

Van de GraaffWall socket
Potential difference100 000 V220 V
Current2 × 10⁻³ A13 A
Time1 × 10⁻³ suntil the fuse blows ≈ 0.1 s
Energy0.5 J286 J

The Van de Graaff has a much higher voltage, but a tiny current for a tiny time, so it delivers very little energy. A socket's current is thousands of times bigger and keeps flowing, which can stop your heart.
